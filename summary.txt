World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Russia and Ukraine began a second day of US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi, their first in nine months; a prisoner swap of up to 1,500 each and Black Sea and energy ceasefires were discussed (Reuters). At COP31 in Antalya, leaders of nearly 200 countries began two days of speeches, with hosts pushing a 35% electricity share of world energy use by 2035; the US stayed away for a second year (Reuters). Millions struck across Spain in its first general strike in 14 years, over housing costs that have nearly doubled rents in a decade, ahead of a 29 November election (Reuters). Anthropic set a $70-$78 price range for its Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, a sale of up to $64.4bn valuing the Claude maker at up to $1.4tn fully diluted (Reuters). October US CPI rose 0.4% against a 0.3% forecast, hotter than expected, and Asian markets fell as traders priced in a December Fed rate rise (Bureau of Labor Statistics). Nvidia shareholders sued the chipmaker and CEO Jensen Huang, alleging they hid his alleged links to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset; China called them baseless; shares fell 4.3% on Monday (Reuters). US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot against RAF Fairford in Britain; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reportedly moved to a military base after threats (BBC). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; he served in NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (Metropolitan Police). The US House voted 211-204 on Tuesday to shelve an effort to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, sending the charges to committee (NPR). A Florida judge ruled President Trump's $10bn defamation suit against the BBC over an edited January 6 speech clip can proceed, dismissing a trade claim (Reuters). Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, with 11,042 confirmed cases and 5,387 deaths, government data showed, the second-largest on record (Reuters).
